Source of revenue (decrease)/ expenditure (decrease):
This bill prohibits the state from accepting and expending federal funds, which
will result in a revenue decrease to the state and commensurate expenditure
decrease beginning in FY 2027. The state of Wyoming receives, appropriates and
expends federal funder in four (4) different methods:
• State appropriations;
• Interim budget authority wherein the Governor authorizes the acceptance of
federal funds;
• Statutory receipt and distribution of federal funds, e.g., Federal Mineral
Royalties (FMRs), Taylor grazing act funds, U.S. Forest Reserve funds; and
• Receipt and expenditure of federal funds by entities outside of the
legislative budget process, e.g., revenues received and expended under the
authority of the University of Wyoming board of trustees, Transportation
Commission, and Game and Fish Commission.
Assumptions:
The above estimates are based upon the average federal funds appropriated or
reported received over the last four fiscal years. The estimates exclude one-time
discretionary funds directed to states wherever possible to provide a broad
estimate of the future revenue and expenditure decrease associated with this
bill, notwithstanding federal revenue provided during the recent pandemic.
Specifically, American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) - State and Local Fiscal Recovery
Funds (SLFRF) - and Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (CARES Act) -
Coronavirus Relief Funds – have been excluded. However, the estimates include
direct federal grants, which may have increased, for pandemic response programs.
Wyoming Department of Transportation FY 2024 estimates and Wyoming Game and Fish
Department FY 2023 and FY 2024 estimates rely upon the budgeted amounts rather
than reported revenues due to timing of audited statements. It is possible that
some federal revenue could be double counted if reported by two different
agencies, e.g., federal revenue received by one agency and granted or transferred
to another.
Over the last four fiscal years, the total federal revenues have ranged from $2.3
billion to $2.9 billion per year, which illustrates the variability based upon
large grants and federal mineral royalty volatility.

NOTICE-AGENCY ESTIMATE OF ADMINISTRATIVE IMPACT REQUESTED
This bill has administrative impact that appears to increase duties or
responsibilities of one or more state agencies and may impact agency spending or
staffing requirements. As introduced, the bill does not modify any state agency
budget or current personnel authorizations.
The following state agencies will be asked to provide their estimate of the
administrative fiscal impact prior to the first committee meeting held to
consider the bill:
State Budget Department (reporting for state agencies and instrumentalities)
University of Wyoming
Community College Commission
Department of Transportation
